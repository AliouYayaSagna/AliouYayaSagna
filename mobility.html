Career project · International mobility

Three places I’d like to study or work.

I want to use my computer science, data and AI skills on projects that are actually useful, and learn from different environments while I do it.

Why go abroad?

At ENSEEIHT I’m studying Digital Sciences, where I’m building skills in AI, Big Data, software, IoT and cybersecurity. Going abroad would let me use them in a different setting, see other ways of working, and better understand how research, business and society connect.

It could be an academic semester, an international internship, a double degree or a collaborative project. The report mentions a minimum of 17 weeks of physical mobility for students enrolled in a study program. What I can actually do depends on the opportunities and mobility agreements the program offers.

01 · Montreal, Canada

University of Montreal / Mila

Project: get better at AI and machine learning in an international research environment.

What I want to learn

Machine learning, deep learning, data models and responsible AI. The University of Montreal and Mila fit my interest in AI research, and in the path of Yoshua Bengio.

What I could do there

  • Take advanced courses in computer science and AI.
  • Join a research project or a lab.
  • Test a model on a real problem.
  • Present my results as a report, a demo or a scientific poster.

Skills I’d build: machine learning, scientific experimentation, data processing, English, and working in an international team.

It would be my first time in a major international AI research environment.

02 · Thies, Senegal

Thies Polytechnic School (EPT)

Project: use my computer science and data skills on a real problem in Senegal.

EPT offers engineering programs in Computer Engineering and Telecommunications, as well as programs in software engineering and artificial intelligence. A mobility here would let me combine what I learn at ENSEEIHT with Senegal’s technological needs.

Project idea

Build a prototype in one of these areas: education, health, transport, agriculture or access to information. It could include data collection, automated processing, a web interface and testing in the field.

Skills I’d build: designing solutions, software development, analyzing needs, adapting to the local context, and working with students and people on the ground.

The report links this project to Ndiaye Dia and Wedji Kane: build technical expertise, then use it to solve something useful nearby.

03 · Cranfield, United Kingdom

Cranfield University

Project: connect artificial intelligence, connected systems and industry.

The report describes Cranfield as focused on applied AI, machine learning, data analysis, autonomous systems and digital transformation. It would show me how technology goes from the lab to industrial use.

What I could do there

  • Design an intelligent system for an industrial case.
  • Work on data, IoT and automation.
  • Join a research or company project.
  • Look at the technical, economic and human effects of a digital solution.

Skills I’d build: intelligent systems, data engineering, IoT, project management, professional English, and working with companies.

The report lists Cranfield as a British partner in ENSEEIHT’s double-degree documents, so it’s a realistic option to look into.

What this says about me

The three destinations have different goals, but together they describe the engineer I want to become: someone who understands technology, knows how to build it, and can adapt it to different contexts.

Montreal · AI researchUnderstand models and experiment with them.

I don’t just want to study somewhere else. I want to come back with experience, new methods and a real project to share with my school and the people I work with.

Next steps

I’ll compare ENSEEIHT’s agreements, application deadlines, available courses and funding, then pick the destination that fits my year of study best.
