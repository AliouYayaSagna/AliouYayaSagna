Career project · Role models

Three people who shaped how I see AI.

Ndiaye Dia, Wedji Kane and Yoshua Bengio each make a difference in their own way: one by building institutions at home, one by linking Senegalese talent to international work, and one by guiding research worldwide.

All resources

Why these three?

Ndiaye Dia and Wedji Kane are Senegalese AI practitioners who co-founded the Institute of Algorithms of Senegal. Yoshua Bengio is one of the best-known researchers in deep learning. Below I go through their paths, the qualities and values I admire in them, and how each one creates impact.

The details come from my career project report and the sources it cites.

Profile 01

Ndiaye Dia

Building digital institutions in Africa

FieldArtificial intelligence, algorithms and knowledge entrepreneurship
OriginKeur Matar Daro, Thies region, Senegal
EducationMathematics and computer science at Cheikh Anta Diop University (2017); École Polytechnique in Paris on a Senegalese government excellence scholarship; AI training.
Main achievementsFounded the Institute of Algorithms of Senegal (2020); started SALTIS (2021); co-founded and directs Jangat.ai.

His path

Dia was drawn to logic and problem-solving, so he studied mathematics and algorithms, then digital sciences and AI at École Polytechnique. He worked on text-analysis models and, remotely, with researchers from the Virtual University of Senegal on COVID-19 modeling. After that he chose to build in Senegal itself: the Institute of Algorithms of Senegal, which aims to make digital knowledge available to more people; SALTIS, a forum on science and careers; and Jangat.ai, a generative AI startup.

He was invited to the UN ECOSOC Youth Forum in 2024 and selected for “Leading the Future 2030.” A 2026 Sika Finance article also mentions his role in the Data and AI department of the Dakar Youth Olympic Games organizing committee.

What I take from his story

  • Qualities: he is persistent, thinks long-term, communicates clearly, and stays humble and generous. He is also good at bringing people together.
  • Values: digital sovereignty, open access to knowledge, and staying rooted in African realities.
  • How he makes an impact: by creating institutions and a startup that give other people tools.
  • What inspires me: coming back home to build can be a strong choice, and obstacles don’t have to decide how things turn out.

According to the report, his education was interrupted twice because of civil-status problems. He saw these obstacles as a reason to push harder.

Profile 02

Wedji Kane

A data engineer linking local talent to global companies

FieldData engineering, AI, digital health and tech entrepreneurship
OriginSenegal; career built in France
EducationS1 baccalaureate with an excellence scholarship; telecommunications engineering at ENSEIRB-MATMECA Bordeaux (2021); AI engineering at Mines Paris–PSL (2022).
RolesData engineer at Onepoint, then Carrefour France; co-founder of the Institute of Algorithms of Senegal; co-founder and managing director of LinkSenAI (founded 2025); general coordinator of SALTIS 2025.

Her path

A 2022 profile described Kane as the first in her S1 exam center to pass the baccalaureate, with an excellence scholarship and a second national prize in mathematics. She went on to an engineering school in France and worked as a data engineer. Along the way she got involved in digital health in Senegal and co-founded the Institute of Algorithms of Senegal.

In 2025 she founded LinkSenAI, which recruits Senegal-based tech and data talent for international digital projects, mostly in Europe. She also coordinated SALTIS in Dakar on November 25–26, 2025.

What I take from her story

  • Qualities: excellent academic results, and the ability to coordinate institutions, startups and researchers.
  • Values: technological sovereignty, inclusion, passing knowledge between generations, and AI that answers real needs such as health.
  • How she makes an impact: by matching local skills with international demand, and showing what this talent can do without pushing it to leave.
  • What inspires me: experience gained abroad can help the country you come from, and women can lead tech companies and major science events.

At the launch of SALTIS 2025, her message was that Senegal should master AI and make it useful, inclusive and accessible, by combining a young team with the experience of those who opened the way.

Profile 03

Yoshua Bengio

A scientific pioneer who now speaks up about the risks

FieldDeep learning, artificial intelligence and AI safety
OriginBorn in Paris in 1964; Canadian citizen
EducationBachelor’s, master’s and doctorate at McGill University
RolesProfessor at the University of Montreal; founder and scientific advisor of Mila; co-founder of Element AI; co-chair and scientific director of the International AI Safety Report.

His path

Bengio shared the 2018 Turing Award with Geoffrey Hinton and Yann LeCun for their foundational work on deep learning. The report mentions his textbook Deep Learning (MIT Press, 2016), his founding of Mila and his later shift toward AI safety. It also points to the International AI Safety Report and to LawZero, a nonprofit launched in 2025 that aims to build capable AI systems that are safe by design.

What I take from his story

  • Qualities: scientific rigor, a huge output of work, a talent for building research communities, and intellectual courage.
  • Values: responsibility of researchers, caution about risks, international cooperation, and science that serves the common good.
  • How he makes an impact: through fundamental research, institution building, and advice to public decision-makers.
  • What inspires me: even at the top of a field, a scientist can change direction to deal with the consequences of a technology they helped create.

His distinctions listed in the report include the 2018 Turing Award, the 2017 Marie-Victorin Prize and the 2019 Killam Prize.

What they have in common

  • All three started with demanding scientific training, but what they do goes beyond personal success.
  • Each one went from expertise to building something for others: institutions, a startup, a forum, a lab or a nonprofit.
  • They see AI as a tool for people and society, not a goal in itself.
  • They care about passing things on: training people, inspiring vocations, connecting generations and building research communities.

Where they differ

  • Dia and Kane focus on Senegal and Africa, while Bengio works on research and safety at a global level.
  • Dia and Kane are still building institutions, while Bengio is changing course after a career already crowned by a Turing Award.
  • Dia and Kane work explicitly for Senegal, whereas Bengio acts as an international scientific voice.
