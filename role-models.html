Three paths in artificial intelligence.

Ndiaye Dia, Wedji Kane and Yoshua Bengio show three complementary ways to create impact: building local institutions, connecting talent with markets and guiding global research.

All resources

Why these three profiles?

Ndiaye Dia and Wedji Kane are Senegalese AI practitioners and co-founders of the Institute of Algorithms of Senegal. Yoshua Bengio is a world-renowned scientist in deep learning. This page presents the main stages, qualities, values and sources of impact described in the report.

The biographical details below reflect the report and its cited sources.

Profile 01

Ndiaye Dia

The builder of African digital institutions

FieldArtificial intelligence, algorithms and knowledge entrepreneurship
OriginKeur Matar Daro, Thies region, Senegal
EducationMathematics and computer science at Cheikh Anta Diop University (2017); École Polytechnique in Paris with a Senegalese government excellence scholarship; AI training.
Key achievementsFounded the Institute of Algorithms of Senegal (2020); initiated SALTIS (2021); co-founded and directs Jangat.ai.

Path and impact

Drawn to logic and problem-solving, Dia studied mathematics and algorithms, then digital sciences and AI at École Polytechnique. He worked on text-analysis models and collaborated remotely with Virtual University of Senegal researchers on COVID-19 modeling. He later chose to build locally: the Institute of Algorithms of Senegal, a center intended to democratize digital knowledge; SALTIS, a forum for science and vocations; and Jangat.ai, a generative AI startup.

The report notes an invitation to the UN ECOSOC Youth Forum in 2024 and selection for “Leading the Future 2030.” It also cites Sika Finance’s 2026 account of his role in the Dakar Youth Olympic Games organizing committee’s Data and AI department.

What stands out in the report

  • Qualities: perseverance, long-term vision, clarity, humility, generosity and ability to bring people together.
  • Values: digital sovereignty, accessible knowledge and grounding in African realities.
  • Lever of impact: creating institutions and a startup that equip others.
  • Inspiration: returning to one’s home country to build can be a powerful path; obstacles do not have to define the outcome.

The report describes two interruptions to his education related to civil-status problems and says he viewed the obstacles as strengthening his determination.

Profile 02

Wedji Kane

The data engineer connecting local talent with global markets

FieldData engineering, AI, digital health and technology entrepreneurship
OriginSenegal; career developed in France
EducationS1 baccalaureate with excellence scholarship; telecommunications engineering at ENSEIRB-MATMECA Bordeaux (2021); AI engineering at Mines Paris–PSL (2022).
Roles citedData engineer at Onepoint, then Carrefour France; co-founder of the Institute of Algorithms of Senegal; co-founder and managing director of LinkSenAI (founded 2025); SALTIS 2025 general coordinator.

Path and impact

A profile published in 2022 described Kane as the first in her S1 examination center to earn the baccalaureate, with an excellence scholarship and a second national prize in mathematics. She continued at an engineering school in France, worked as a data engineer and became involved in digital health in Senegal while co-founding the Institute of Algorithms of Senegal.

In 2025, she founded LinkSenAI, which recruits Senegal-based technology and data talent for international digital projects, particularly in Europe. She also coordinated SALTIS in Dakar on November 25–26, 2025.

What stands out in the report

  • Qualities: strong academic performance and coordination across institutions, startups and researchers.
  • Values: technological sovereignty, inclusion, intergenerational transmission and AI serving concrete needs such as health.
  • Lever of impact: connecting local skills with international demand while showcasing talent without forcing it to leave.
  • Inspiration: experience gained abroad can become a resource for one’s country of origin; women can lead technology companies and major science events.

The report records her message at the launch of SALTIS 2025: Senegal should master AI and make it useful, inclusive and accessible, combining a young team with the experience of those who opened the way.

Profile 03

Yoshua Bengio

The scientific pioneer who became a voice for caution

FieldDeep learning, artificial intelligence and AI safety
OriginBorn in Paris in 1964; Canadian citizen
EducationBachelor’s, master’s and doctoral degrees at McGill University
Roles citedProfessor at the University of Montreal; founder and scientific advisor of Mila; co-founder of Element AI; co-chair and scientific director of the International AI Safety Report.

Path and impact

Bengio shared the 2018 Turing Award with Geoffrey Hinton and Yann LeCun for foundational deep-learning work. The report cites his textbook Deep Learning (MIT Press, 2016), his founding of Mila and his later turn toward AI safety. It notes the International AI Safety Report and the 2025 launch of LawZero, a nonprofit aiming to design capable AI systems that are safe by design.

What stands out in the report

  • Qualities: scientific rigor, productivity, research-community building and intellectual courage.
  • Values: researcher responsibility, caution about risks, international cooperation and science serving the common good.
  • Lever of impact: fundamental research, institution building and influence on public decision-makers.
  • Inspiration: a scientist at the top of a field can change direction to address the consequences of the technology they helped create.

The report lists distinctions including the 2018 Turing Award, the 2017 Marie-Victorin Prize and the 2019 Killam Prize.

Common points

  • Demanding scientific training is a starting point, but their work goes beyond individual achievement.
  • Each moves from expertise to building something for others: institutions, a startup, a forum, a laboratory or a nonprofit.
  • They frame AI as a tool for people and society, not an end in itself.
  • Transmission matters: training people, awakening vocations, connecting generations and building research communities.

Key differences

  • Dia and Kane focus on local and continental impact; Bengio has a global research and safety role.
  • Dia and Kane are building institutions, while Bengio is redirecting a career already recognized by a Turing Award.
  • Dia and Kane act explicitly for Senegal; Bengio operates as an international scientific voice.
